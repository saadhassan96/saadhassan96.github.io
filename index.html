I study and design human–AI systems that make communication, information, and public services accessible.

Examples include sign language and captioning technologies, reading supports, accessible visualizations, and civic dashboards. This work centers the experiences of end users, including people with disabilities and their communities.

Google Scholar

I am an Assistant Professor in the School of Science and Engineering at Tulane University, where I direct the NOLA A11y Lab and am affiliated with the Tulane Center for Community-Engaged AI. My research spans accessible computing, human-computer interaction, and computational social science, and is supported by the National Science Foundation, the Louisiana Board of Regents, and internal awards from Tulane. I have published over 30 papers in HCI and accessibility venues such as CHI, ASSETS, TACCESS, and MobileHCI, and in AI venues such as CVPR, NeurIPS, and EMNLP Findings. Four of them received Best Paper nominations or Honorable Mentions at CHI and ASSETS. I earned my Ph.D. in Computing and Information Sciences at the Rochester Institute of Technology, advised by Matt Huenerfauth, with support from a Duolingo Dissertation Research Grant. I serve on the program committees for ASSETS and CHI.
