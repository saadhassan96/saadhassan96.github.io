I study and design human–AI systems that make communication, information, and public services accessible.

Examples include sign language and captioning technologies, reading supports, accessible visualizations, and civic dashboards. This work centers the experiences of end users, including people with disabilities and their communities.

Google Scholar NOLA A11y Lab CEAI

I am an Assistant Professor in the School of Science and Engineering at Tulane University, where I direct the NOLA A11y Lab, co-direct the Human-Centered Computing (HCC) Group, and am affiliated with the Tulane Center for Community-Engaged AI (CEAI). My research spans human-computer interaction, human-AI interaction, and accessibility, and is supported by the National Science Foundation, the Louisiana Board of Regents, and internal awards from Tulane. I have published over 35 papers in HCI and accessibility venues such as CHI, ASSETS, TACCESS, and MobileHCI, and in AI venues such as CVPR, NeurIPS, and EMNLP Findings. Four of them received Best Paper nominations or Honorable Mentions at CHI and ASSETS. I earned my Ph.D. in Computing and Information Sciences at the Rochester Institute of Technology, advised by Matt Huenerfauth, and received a Duolingo Dissertation Research Grant. During my Ph.D., I was a research intern and student researcher at Google Research and Meta Reality Labs. I serve on the program committees for ASSETS and CHI.
